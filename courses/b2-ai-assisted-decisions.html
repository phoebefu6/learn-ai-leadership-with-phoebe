AI-assisted decisions: think wider, then decide

Decisions are the core of the job, and AI is a genuine force multiplier on them - if you use it to widen your thinking, not to rubber-stamp it. This session gives you the decision-quality method, then a live simulator where you watch a decision go from a confident guess to a decision-grade brief as you frame it, widen the options, and stress-test for bias. The call, always, stays yours.

Part 0

Decision quality, not decision outcome

A good decision is not one that happened to work out - it is one made well: the right question, real options, the information you had, and a clear values call. You control the process, not the luck. AI can lift the quality of the process dramatically - widening options, surfacing information, running a pre-mortem - while the framing and the values stay yours. Tonight you build that habit and feel it in the simulator.

Part 1 · covers decision-science + MIT Sloan decision frameworks

What makes a decision good 6 min live

Five ingredients make a decision high-quality: the right frame, real options, sound information, a clear values call, and commitment. AI can genuinely help with options and information, and can stress-test the frame - but the frame and the values are the leader's. Anatomy first.

LiveFrame first - the highest-leverage move3 min▶

Most bad decisions are answers to the wrong question. "Should we do X?" is usually a symptom-level frame; "what is the best way to solve the problem X is one response to?" opens the real decision. AI is quick to answer whatever you ask - so the quality of your frame sets the ceiling on the quality of its help.

  • Reframe from binary to open. "X or not" hides the third option that is often the best.
  • Name the real problem. The decision is rarely the first thing you reached for; it is the goal underneath it.
  • You frame, AI fills. Give it the real frame and it will surface options and information; give it a bad frame and it confidently optimizes the wrong thing.
★ Try it now - reframe a decisionHere is a decision I am facing: [it]. Before giving any answer, help me reframe it: what is the underlying problem, what is the real question, and what binary am I trapped in that might be hiding a better third option?
Real world

"Should I fire this vendor?" was the wrong question. A manager framed it as fire-or-keep. Reframed with AI to "how do we get reliable delivery for this workstream?", a third option appeared - restructure the SLA and split one critical piece in-house - that beat both. Same facts, a better frame, a better decision.

Self-studyThe pre-mortem - the cheapest risk tool you have2 min read▶

A pre-mortem asks: assume it is six months later and this decision failed - why? It surfaces the risks optimism hides, and AI is genuinely good at it because it is not emotionally invested in your plan. Run one on every consequential decision.

Ask AI for...What it surfaces
The three most likely failure modesRisks your optimism skipped
Whose perspective is missingStakeholders you did not consult
What would change your recommendationThe evidence you should go get
Part 2 · the decision-quality simulator

Drive it yourself: watch the decision get better ★ 8 min · everyone plays

Here is the whole lesson in one control panel. The AI is helping with a management decision. Every lever you turn on raises the quality - frame the real decision, add context, widen the options, stress-test for bias, apply your judgment. Watch decision quality climb and flip to the scorecard to see how many decision types come out sound. Notice what never moves the needle: letting AI decide for you.

What the simulator is teaching Start with everything off: the AI gives a confident, agreeable push toward whatever you hinted at. Frame the real decision and it sharpens; add context, options, and a bias check and it becomes genuine counsel; apply your judgment and it hands the call back to you, where it belongs. Quality came from the process - and the human owning it.
Demo 1 of 2

Build a decision brief for a real call ★ 12 min · everyone builds

Now do it for real, on a decision you actually face. This is the reusable pattern you will run before any consequential call.

Pick a real, consequential decision. Strip names and people-data (rail two).

Frame it as the underlying problem, then ask AI for three genuine options with tradeoffs, including a do-nothing baseline.

Ask for the strongest case against your favorite, a pre-mortem, and whose perspective is missing - the full advisor treatment.

Now write your decision and the why in two sentences. AI built the brief; you make and own the call.

Save the prompt as your "decision brief" template. Every hard call gets ten better minutes now.

★ The decision-brief promptHelp me make a high-quality decision. The underlying problem is: [problem]. Act as a candid advisor who disagrees when warranted. 1) Reframe the real question if I have framed it too narrowly. 2) Give three genuine options with tradeoffs, including doing nothing. 3) Make the strongest case against the option I am leaning toward: [option]. 4) Run a pre-mortem: if this fails in six months, the likeliest reasons. 5) Tell me whose perspective I am missing and what evidence would change your view. Do not tell me I am right by default. I will make the final call.
Demo 2 of 2

Red-team a decision you have already made ★ 8 min · build your own

The habit that separates good managers from lucky ones: pressure-test a decision before it ships, not after it fails.

Take a decision you are about to act on. Give AI the plan and ask it to be a hostile red-team, not a supporter.

Ask for the three ways this backfires, the assumption most likely to be wrong, and the one change that would most reduce the risk.

Decide what to adjust - or consciously accept the risk. Either way you now lead with your eyes open.

★ The red-team promptHere is a decision I am about to act on: [plan]. Do not reassure me. Be a hostile red-team: the three most likely ways this backfires, the single assumption most likely to be wrong, the stakeholder who will be most unhappy and why, and the one change that would most reduce the downside. Rank them by likelihood times impact.
Real world

The pre-mortem that saved a launch. A team red-teamed a rollout plan with AI the day before. The top-ranked failure - a dependency no one owned - was exactly what would have sunk it. Ten minutes of deliberate pessimism beat a month of clean-up. The AI did not decide anything; it just refused to be agreeable when it counted.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Run the decision-brief prompt on one real (anonymized) call this week. Note the option or risk you had not seen.
  • Play the simulator again and predict the quality score before each toggle - predicting it means you understand what drives a good decision.
  • Red-team one decision you have already made. If it holds up, good; if not, you just avoided a slow failure.
  • Catch one binary frame this week ("X or not") and force a third option before deciding.
  • Optional: keep a two-line decision log - the call and the why - so you can judge your process, not just the outcome.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · In the simulator, what raises the decision quality?

Quality comes from the process: frame, options, information, bias check, and human judgment. Letting AI decide never improves it.

2 · What is the highest-leverage move in a decision?

The frame sets the ceiling on everything after it. A bad frame makes AI confidently optimize the wrong thing; a good frame unlocks the real options.

3 · What is a pre-mortem?

A pre-mortem surfaces the risks optimism hides. AI is good at it precisely because it is not emotionally invested in your plan.
