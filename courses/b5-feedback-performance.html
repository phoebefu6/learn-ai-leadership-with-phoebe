Feedback & performance: AI structures it, you own the call

Feedback is where AI is most tempting and most dangerous. It will happily draft a review, structure hard feedback, and generate coaching questions - and it will just as happily launder a biased pattern into a confident-sounding verdict about a real person. Tonight you learn to use AI for the structure and the words, while the assessment, the fairness, and the human moment stay entirely yours. Nobody's real data goes near a consumer tool.

Part 0

The hard frame before we start

Say it before you open a chat window: AI structures feedback; you own the judgment, the fairness, and the human moment. A review is not a document - it is a decision about a person's livelihood and growth. AI can help you say it clearly and fairly, but it must never make the assessment, and a real employee's data must never touch a consumer tool. This session is the loudest the two rails will ever be, because people-decisions are where AI does the most quiet damage.

Part 1 · covers management + HR feedback canon

AI structures, you judge 7 min live

AI is genuinely good at three feedback jobs: shaping a raw observation into the Situation-Behavior-Impact frame, drafting a review from evidence you supply, and generating coaching questions that open a conversation. What it cannot do is the part that matters - assess the person, judge what is fair, and be present when it lands. Those stay human, every time.

LiveThe one that laundered a bias - why this rail is loud4 min▶

AI can take a biased pattern and hand it back to you as a calm, well-written, confident verdict. That is the danger: it does not remove bias, it dresses it up. If your notes lean on "not a culture fit" or reward the loudest voice, the AI will polish that into a fair-sounding review - and you will trust it more because it reads well. The assessment has to be yours, made with fairness front of mind.

  • AI inherits your framing. Feed it thin or skewed notes and it produces a confident, skewed review. Garbage in, eloquent garbage out.
  • Fluency feels like fairness. A well-written verdict reads as objective. It is not. Polish is not proof.
  • A human owns every call about a person. Rating, promotion, PIP - AI drafts words, never decides the outcome.
★ Try it now (any chat AI) - make it hunt your biasHere are my anonymized notes on a team member's quarter: [paste]. Before you draft anything, tell me where these notes might be biased or unfair - what is evidence vs opinion, what is missing, and whether I am rewarding personality over results.
Real world

The review that read beautifully and was wrong. A manager fed rough, frustrated notes about a quiet engineer into a chatbot and asked for a performance summary. It came back polished, confident, and quietly damning - "struggles to demonstrate impact". Only when a peer asked "what did they actually ship?" did it turn out the person had carried two releases. The AI did not judge the engineer. It made the manager's blind spot sound like a finding.

Self-studyThe three feedback jobs AI is good at2 min read▶
  • Structure into SBI. You saw something; AI shapes it into Situation, Behavior, Impact so the feedback is specific and about actions, not character.
  • Draft from your evidence. You supply the facts and examples; AI drafts the review. It writes from what you give it - it does not fill gaps with guesses about the person.
  • Generate coaching questions. AI is strong at open, non-leading questions that help a person reflect - the opposite of telling them what to think.

Notice the pattern: in all three, you bring the substance and the judgment; AI brings structure and words. That split is the whole session.

Three concrete moves, each with a guardrail. SBI drafting keeps feedback about behavior, not the person. Review drafting works only from evidence you supply, never the model's guesses. Coaching questions open the door instead of pushing through it. The table pairs each use with the fairness guardrail that keeps it honest.

Self-studySBI in one minute - the frame AI drafts to2 min read▶

SBI keeps feedback fair by keeping it factual. Three parts:

  • Situation - when and where, concretely. "In yesterday's client review..." not "you always...".
  • Behavior - what the person did, observable and specific. What a camera would have seen, not your interpretation of their intent.
  • Impact - the effect it had on the work, the team, or the client. The "so what" that makes it worth saying.
Why AI is good at this Managers tend to give vague, character-flavored feedback under time pressure. AI is patient and structured, so it is a reliable SBI coach - as long as you supply real, specific observations for it to shape.
Part 3 · the people-data + fairness rail, loud

The lines you do not cross 4 min live

Everything in this course keeps the judgment human. In feedback and performance the lines are sharper than anywhere else, because a mistake here lands on someone's career. Four of them, and none are negotiable.

LiveRail 1 here - AI drafts words, never decides outcomes2 min▶

AI never decides a rating, a promotion, or a PIP. It can structure the words once you have made the call, but the call - and the accountability for it - is yours. "The AI rated them a 3" is not a defensible sentence to your employee, your HR partner, or yourself.

  • Ratings, promotions, PIPs are human decisions. Made by you, with evidence, with fairness in mind. AI is downstream of the decision, drafting the write-up.
  • Check for bias and consistency across the team. Are you applying the same standard to everyone? AI can help you audit that - it cannot be trusted to have done it for you.
Real world

Don't let the tool carry the hard moment. A manager scripted an entire feedback conversation with AI and read it near-verbatim. The person felt handled, not heard, and the trust took months to rebuild. Use AI to prepare - then close the laptop and be present. The other person can always tell.

LiveRail 2 here - no real employee data in consumer tools2 min▶

No employee names, performance records, or sensitive HR information goes into a consumer chatbot - ever. Employment law and data-protection law (GDPR, PDPA) apply to your team's data exactly as they apply to a customer's. And where your org requires it, disclose that AI helped draft.

  • Anonymize to think. Strip names and identifying details before you paste. "A senior engineer who missed two deadlines" is safe; a real name and record is not.
  • Real data, approved tools only. Anything identifiable about an employee lives in enterprise or HR-sanctioned systems, never a free assistant.
  • Disclose where required. If your org or law asks you to note that AI assisted with a review, do it. Transparency protects you and the person.
Say it out loud once "AI structures the feedback; I own the assessment, the fairness, and the moment - and no real person's data goes near a consumer tool." That sentence is this whole session.
Demo 1 of 2

Draft SBI feedback, then edit for fairness ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Take a real situation, anonymize it hard, and use AI to shape it into clean SBI feedback. Then do the part that matters: edit it for fairness and specificity until it is honest, kind, and unmistakably yours.

Pick a real piece of feedback you owe someone. Strip every name and identifying detail - roles and behaviors only (rail two).

Ask AI to shape your raw observation into SBI: situation, behavior, impact. Give it specific facts, not adjectives.

Read the draft as the person receiving it. Is it about behavior or character? Specific or vague? Fair, or is my frustration leaking in?

Edit hard. Cut anything the AI invented, sharpen the impact, and make sure the behavior is something the person can actually change.

Ask AI to red-team your edited version for bias - then judge its answer yourself. You own the final words and the fairness.

Prompt A - the trap (asking AI to judge the person)Here are my notes on a team member. Are they a strong performer or a weak one, and what rating should I give?
★ Prompt B - the move (AI structures, you judge)Help me give clear, fair feedback. Here is an anonymized situation: [a senior engineer missed two committed deadlines this sprint; the second slipped a client demo; they did not flag the risk early]. Shape this into SBI feedback - Situation, Behavior, Impact - that is specific, about behavior not character, and something they can act on. Then flag anything that reads as unfair or as my interpretation rather than an observable fact. Do not assign a rating or judge whether they are a good employee - that is my call.
Why this is demo one SBI is the reusable spine of every hard conversation. Learn to make AI draft it and yourself edit it for fairness, and feedback stops being the thing you avoid until review season.
Demo 2 of 2

Prep a coaching conversation - and a pre-mortem ★ 8 min · build your own

Feedback is a moment; coaching is a conversation. Use AI to prepare open questions and to pre-mortem how the talk could go wrong - so you walk in ready for the hard turns and own the room as a human.

Describe the anonymized coaching goal: what you want the person to grow into, and what is getting in the way.

Ask AI for a handful of open, non-leading coaching questions - the kind that help them reflect, not ones that corner them into your answer.

Ask for a pre-mortem: if this conversation goes badly, what are the three most likely ways, and how do I stay steady in each?

Pick the two questions that fit your person and drop the rest. Prep is preparation, not a script - close the laptop before you walk in.

★ The coaching-prep promptHelp me prepare for a coaching conversation. Anonymized context: [a capable team member is ready for more scope but hangs back in cross-team meetings and defers when they should push their view]. Give me: (1) five open, non-leading questions to help them reflect - not questions that steer them to my conclusion; (2) a pre-mortem - the three most likely ways this conversation goes wrong and how I stay calm and curious in each; (3) one thing I should listen for. I own the conversation - I just want to walk in prepared.
Real world

The pre-mortem that saved the conversation. A manager prepped a growth talk and asked AI to war-game how it could go sideways. Top risk: the person hears "you are not ready" instead of "I see more in you". Forewarned, the manager opened with the belief, not the gap. The person leaned in. Same message, opposite outcome - because the prep surfaced the failure mode before the room did.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish both demos if you did not complete them live - especially editing the SBI draft for fairness. The edit is the lesson, not the draft.
  • Deliver one piece of the SBI feedback you built to a real person this week, in a real conversation. Notice how specificity changes how it lands.
  • Before your next review cycle, run the bias-check prompt on your anonymized notes for two people and compare - are you applying the same bar?
  • Confirm which tools you are cleared to use for anything touching employee data. If it is "none yet", keep every real name out of consumer tools until it is settled.
  • Optional: catch yourself once about to ask AI "what rating should I give?" and reframe it as "help me structure the evidence - I will make the call".
