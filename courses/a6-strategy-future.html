Part 1 · covers 2026 exec-ed (agentic) · re-verify

The agentic organization, realistically 6 min live

2026 exec-ed programs now add agentic AI leadership as a first-class topic, and the hype has run ahead of the reality. A leader's job is to hold the honest line. What is real inside 24 months: AI-augmented teams and governed agents running defined, bounded workflows. What is hype: the fully autonomous organization that runs itself. This moves fast - re-verify the specifics before you commit a board to them.

LiveReal vs hype - hold the honest line3 min▶

The fastest way to lose credibility is to sell a future you cannot deliver. Agentic AI is real and worth planning for - but the version that is real in 24 months is bounded: agents that run specific, governed workflows under human oversight, and teams that work alongside them. The autonomous organization that runs itself is a keynote, not a roadmap. Leaders who conflate the two set expectations they will have to walk back.

  • Real in 24 months. AI-augmented teams doing real work, and governed agents handling defined workflows with clear boundaries and human oversight.
  • Hype in 24 months. The autonomous org, agents making unbounded decisions, humans out of the loop. Compelling on stage, unaccountable in practice.
  • Re-verify before you commit. The agentic timeline shifts quarter to quarter. Check the current state before you put dates in front of a board.
Real world

The roadmap that promised autonomy and delivered a walk-back. A leader pitched a board on an autonomous, self-running operation inside two years. Twelve months in, the governed-workflow reality did not match the keynote, and the credibility hit was worse than the delay. The honest version - augmented teams now, bounded agents later - would have landed and held.

Questions to ask your team "What is genuinely real for us in 24 months, versus what did we borrow from a keynote?" · "Where would a bounded, governed agent add real value?" · "Have we re-verified the timeline before promising it?"
Self-studyWhat "governed agent" actually means2 min read▶

A governed agent is not an autonomous employee. It is software that executes a defined workflow within explicit boundaries, with a human accountable for the outcome - the rails from every prior session, applied to a more capable tool. The word doing the work is "governed", not "agentic". Strip the governance and you do not have a strategy, you have a liability.

  • Bounded workflow. A specific job with a clear start, end, and scope - not open-ended authority to act across the business.
  • Human accountable. Rail one still holds: the agent executes, a named human owns the outcome. Agentic does not repeal accountability.
  • Oversight and guardrails. Monitoring, limits, and a stop button. The more capable the tool, the more the governance from session a4 matters, not less.
Questions to ask your team "For any agent we plan, what are its boundaries - and who owns the outcome?" · "Do the rails from a4 hold as the tools get more capable?" · "Where is the stop button, and who can press it?"
Part 2 · covers exec-ed transformation sequencing

Sequencing the transformation 5 min live

Transformations stall for a predictable reason: leaders skip the foundation and jump to the exciting part. The sequence that works is three phases, in order. Foundation - literacy, culture, and policy. Then adoption - augmented teams doing real work. Then transformation - agentic operating models. Skip the foundation and the whole thing sits on sand.

LiveFoundation, then adoption, then transformation3 min▶

Each phase earns the next. Foundation makes the organization able to adopt at all - people who are AI-literate, a culture safe enough to experiment, and policy that says what is allowed. Adoption is augmented teams changing how work actually gets done, with honest ROI. Only then does transformation - reshaping operating models around governed agents - have solid ground to stand on. Reverse the order and you get impressive pilots on top of an organization that cannot sustain them.

  • Foundation first. Literacy, culture, and policy - the sessions before this one. Unglamorous, and the reason everything after it holds.
  • Then adoption. Augmented teams, real workflow change, measured ROI - the whole of session a5. This is where value first shows up.
  • Then transformation. Agentic operating models, reshaped around what is now proven. Last, not first - because it depends on the two below it.
Real world

The transformation that stalled on skipped foundations. An organization jumped straight to an ambitious agentic operating model - no literacy, thin culture, no policy. The pilots dazzled and then collapsed, because the people could not sustain them and no one knew the rules. Rebuilt from the foundation up, the same ambition finally held. Sequence was the whole difference.

Questions to ask your team "Which phase are we honestly in - foundation, adoption, or transformation?" · "Are we skipping foundation because it is less exciting?" · "What proof do we have that we have earned the next phase?"
Self-studyWhy skipping foundation is why transformations stall2 min read▶

The stall is almost never the technology. It is an organization asked to run before it could walk - agentic ambition on top of people who were never made literate, a culture that punishes the experiments the change requires, and no policy to draw the lines. Foundation is slow and invisible, which is exactly why leaders under pressure skip it, and exactly why the transformation then fails to hold.

  • Foundation is invisible, so it gets cut. Literacy and culture do not demo well, so they lose to the flashy pilot - until the flashy pilot has nothing under it.
  • Adoption proves you are ready. If augmented teams cannot make real ROI stick, the organization is not ready for agentic operating models. Adoption is the honest gate.
  • Patience is the strategy. The leaders who win sequence deliberately and resist the pressure to skip ahead. Slower foundation, faster transformation.
Questions to ask your team "What is the pressure pushing us to skip, and what will it cost later?" · "Has adoption actually proven ROI before we scale to transformation?" · "Are we patient enough to build the foundation right?"
Part 3 · the story that rallies people

The leadership narrative 4 min live

A roadmap on a slide informs; a narrative moves people. The story that rallies a team through an AI transformation has five beats: where we are, the bet, the guardrails, the people, and the milestones. Told honestly - especially the people beat - it is the difference between a team that follows and a team that complies.

LiveThe five beats of the narrative2 min▶

People do not rally to a Gantt chart. They rally to a story that tells them where they stand, what the organization is betting on, how it will keep them safe, what it means for them, and how they will know it is working. Miss the people beat and the whole story reads as spin. Told straight, it earns the trust that a transformation runs on.

  • Where we are. The honest present - what AI already does for us and what it does not. No hype, no doom.
  • The bet. What we are choosing to invest in and why now. Clear enough that a skeptic understands the logic.
  • The guardrails. The rails from this whole track - AI advises, humans decide and own it, people-data protected. Guardrails are reassurance, not red tape.
  • The people. Augment, not replace - said honestly. If reskilling is the plan, the plan is the proof. This is the beat that earns or loses the room.
  • The milestones. The 24-month roadmap made concrete - how we and they will know it is working, honestly measured.
Questions to ask your team "Does our story have all five beats, or does it skip the people one?" · "Is the augment-not-replace beat honest, or is it spin?" · "Would a skeptic on the team find this credible?"
Self-studyHonesty is the load-bearing beat2 min read▶

The people beat is where most AI narratives quietly break. "We're augmenting, not replacing" is a beautiful line and a dangerous one if it is not true. People can tell the difference between a promise and a slogan, and once they catch a leader dressing up a cut as an upgrade, every other beat loses its credibility. The narrative works because it is honest, or it does not work at all.

  • Say the true thing. If it is augment, prove it with the reskilling investment. If some roles genuinely change, say so with respect - do not launder it.
  • The guardrails make it safe to believe. The rails from a1 through a5 are what let people trust the story. Naming them is not bureaucracy, it is the reassurance.
  • Milestones keep it honest over time. A narrative with measurable milestones cannot drift into spin - the numbers keep the story accountable to reality.
Questions to ask your team "Where are we tempted to say something that is not quite true?" · "Do our guardrails actually reassure, or just sound like rules?" · "Will the milestones keep us honest a year from now?"
Your turn

Sketch a 24-month roadmap and the narrative ◐ 20 min · leaders

The finale artifact. Sketch your own 24-month roadmap across the three phases, then write the five narrative beats you would actually say to your team. Grounded, honest, and yours - the thing you leave the whole leader track holding.

  • Sketch the roadmap in three phases. Foundation, adoption, transformation - across now, 6, 12, and 24 months. Keep the 24-month scope realistic: augmented teams and governed workflows, not the self-running org.
  • Mark what is real vs hype. For each milestone, is this genuinely buildable, or borrowed from a keynote? Re-verify anything agentic before you would commit it.
  • Write the five narrative beats. Where we are, the bet, the guardrails, the people, the milestones - in the words you would use out loud, not slide-speak.
  • Pressure-test the people beat. Is your augment-not-replace beat honest? If reskilling is the plan, is the investment in the roadmap to prove it?
  • Say it out loud once. Read the narrative aloud. If a beat rings hollow or hypey to your own ear, it will to your team - fix it before they hear it.
Questions to ask your team "Is the 24-month scope real, or did we let hype set the dates?" · "Does the sequence respect foundation-first?" · "Would this narrative rally the room, or just inform it?"
