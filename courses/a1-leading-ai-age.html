Part 1 · covers MIT Sloan / Wharton AI-leadership foundations

What actually changes for leaders 6 min live

AI does not replace the leader - it moves where the leader spends time. As routine production drops, the work that is left is the work that was always the point: judgment when the data is silent, direction when the path is unclear, and presence with the people who carry it out. The mandate that follows is not "use AI the most". It is "lead the adoption well".

LiveThe mix of your week moves toward the human work3 min▶

Picture your week as a mix. AI takes a bite out of the routine production - the first draft, the summary, the status roll-up. What it hands back is time, and time flows to the parts of leading that never automated: deciding, pointing the way, and being present with people. That is a gift and a demand at once, because those parts are harder and more exposed than production ever was.

  • Less production. Drafting, summarizing, and first-pass synthesis get faster. Good - that was never where you added the most value.
  • More judgment and direction. The freed time surfaces the calls only you can make: what matters, what to trade off, where to point the organization.
  • More people, not less. As AI handles more of the words, the trust, the reading of a room, and the hard conversation matter more, not less.
Real world

The leader who mistook activity for leadership. An executive proudly automated every report and update, then wondered why the team felt adrift. AI had cleared the production, but nobody had used the freed time to set direction or be present. The tool did its part; the leadership gap was the part a human had quietly stopped doing.

★ Questions to ask your team: Where is AI already saving us time - and are we spending that time on better judgment and more presence, or just doing more of the same faster?

Part 2 · covers the AI-leadership maturity arc

The three eras of leader-AI 5 min live

Leaders climb a short ladder with AI. First you use it yourself to draft and think. Then you enable your team - coaching them and shaping the workflows. Then, at the top, you reshape how the organization operates around agentic models. Most leaders today are on rungs one and two, and that is exactly where they should be - each rung is earned, not skipped.

LivePersonal use, team enablement, org transformation3 min▶

The eras are not skill levels for a tool - they are widening circles of leadership. Each one demands more of you as a leader and less as an operator.

  • Era 1 - personal use. You use AI to draft, structure, and think through your own decisions. The value is a sharper you. This is where you feel the tool honestly, including its habit of agreeing with you.
  • Era 2 - team enablement. You stop being the power user and start being the coach: you shape the workflows, set the guardrails, and help your team use AI well and safely. Your leverage is the team's competence, not your own.
  • Era 3 - org transformation. You redesign how work flows when agents and people operate together - the operating model, the cadence, the measures. This is a strategy job, and it is the far horizon of this course.
Don't skip a rung A leader who jumps to "transform the org" without personally feeling what AI does well and badly tends to buy the hype and mandate tools nobody trusts. Earn rung two by living rung one first.

★ Questions to ask your team: Honestly, which rung is each of us on - and what would it take to move our whole team up one, together, in the next quarter?

Self-studyWhere most leaders actually are2 min read▶

If you are somewhere between era one and era two, you are exactly where most leaders are in 2026 - and that is not behind. The organizations getting real value are the ones enabling teams well, not the ones chasing agentic transformation before their people trust the tool.

  • Rung one is not remedial. Using AI to sharpen your own decisions is genuine leadership work - session a2 is entirely about doing it well.
  • Rung two is where the leverage is. Most of the course lives here: culture (a3), ethics (a4), and change (a5) are all about enabling a team, not a tool.
  • Rung three is a horizon, not a deadline. Session a6 looks at agentic operating models. Aim at it; do not pretend you are there.
Part 3 · the map of the whole course

The leader's real job, and the two rails 4 min live

Strip away the tool and the job is what it always was, made sharper by AI: steer the decisions, shape the culture, hold the ethics, lead the change, and set the strategy. Those are the next five sessions. Running underneath all of them are two rails that never bend.

LiveFive fronts - your map to the rest of the course2 min▶

Everything a leader does with AI lands on one of five fronts. Each is a session, and each keeps the human in the seat that matters.

  • Steer decisions (a2). Use AI to widen options and stress-test, then make the call yourself. Decision quality is judged by the process, not the outcome.
  • Shape culture (a3). Build the trust, safety, and permission to experiment that let a team adopt AI without fear or recklessness.
  • Hold ethics (a4). Own accountability, watch for bias, and keep transparency - especially wherever AI touches a decision about a person.
  • Lead change (a5). Move people through resistance and the adoption curve, and prove the ROI without overselling.
  • Set strategy (a6). Look ahead to agentic operating models and a realistic roadmap for the next 24 months.

★ Questions to ask your team: Of these five fronts - decisions, culture, ethics, change, strategy - which one is our weakest link on AI right now, and who owns fixing it?

LiveThe two rails - say them plainly2 min▶

These two rails are not technique. They are the line between a leader who gets sharper with AI and one who quietly hands away the parts of the job that were the point. Every later session assumes them.

  • Rail 1 - AI advises, the leader decides and owns it. The judgment, the accountability, and the human impact of a decision never transfer to the model. "The AI recommended it" is not leadership. Do not outsource your judgment or your empathy - the two things AI cannot do are the two things leadership is made of.
  • Rail 2 - protect people-data. Never put employee names, performance details, or sensitive HR information into consumer AI tools, and stay alert to bias whenever AI touches a decision about a person - hiring, promotion, performance. Employment and data-protection law (GDPR, PDPA) apply to your team's data exactly as they do to customers'.
Say it out loud once "AI advises, I decide and own it, and I protect my people's data." That sentence is the spine of the whole leader track.
Between now and a2

Map where AI belongs in your week ◐ 20-30 min · bring it to a2

A thinking exercise, no tool required. The point is to see your own week the way this session frames it - production that AI can take, and human work that only you can do - so you arrive at a2 with a real decision to sharpen.

  • List the recurring blocks of your typical week - reports, updates, one-to-ones, decisions, planning, firefighting.
  • Tag each one: AI can help here (draft, synthesize, widen) or the leader adds the value (judgment, direction, people, ownership).
  • For the "AI can help" blocks, ask: if AI freed this time, where would I want it to go - and be honest if the answer is "more of the same faster".
  • Name the single most important decision you are facing this quarter. Anonymize any people-data. Bring it to a2 - you will use it to practice the AI-augmented decision.
  • Note which rung of the ladder you are on today, and one concrete move to climb toward the next.
