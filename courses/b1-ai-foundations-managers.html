Part 1 · covers MIT Sloan / Wharton AI-leadership foundations

What AI actually is - for a manager 6 min live

A large language model predicts the most likely next words. For a manager that makes it a superb thinking partner - it drafts, structures, synthesizes, and widens your options in seconds - and a dangerous one, because it is confident, agreeable, and blind to the people in front of you. It will help you think; it must never think for you.

LiveThe agreeableness trap - why AI is a bad yes-man3 min▶

A good advisor tells you when you are wrong. AI, tuned to be helpful and agreeable, tends to tell you that you are right - it will rationalize the decision you clearly already want. For a manager that is uniquely dangerous, because the whole value of counsel is friction, and AI removes it. The fix is to make it argue against you on purpose.

  • It agrees by default. Ask "is this a good idea?" and you will usually hear yes. Confidence is not endorsement.
  • Over-reliance erodes judgment. The more you let it decide, the less your own judgment gets exercised - a muscle that atrophies.
  • The fix is adversarial prompting. "Argue the other side", "give me the strongest case against this", "what am I missing" - make AI the challenger, not the cheerleader.
★ Try it now (any chat AI) - feel it flipHere is a decision I am leaning toward: [describe it]. First tell me why it is a good idea. Now give me the three strongest arguments AGAINST it, and what would have to be true for me to be wrong.
Real world

The reorg the AI cheered all the way to failure. A manager talked through a reorg with a chatbot; it agreed at every turn, polished the rationale, and drafted the announcement. Nobody in the loop played skeptic. The plan shipped and stalled on the exact objection a challenger would have raised. AI did not make the call - but by only agreeing, it helped a bad one feel certain.

The manager AI tool landscape, 2026 5 min live

The right first question is not "which tool" but "is this safe for the people-data I am about to put in it?" Three layers, and the line between them is about confidentiality and the people you lead, not raw quality.

LiveGeneral, in-suite, and the people-data line3 min▶

Each layer has a job. Do the thinking anywhere; put the people-data only where it is safe.

  • General assistants (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) - your thinking partner for decisions, drafts, synthesis, and playing skeptic. Keep the input anonymized.
  • In-suite copilots + notetakers - Copilot in M365/Teams, meeting notetakers; convenient, and governed by that tool's data terms. Know what they capture and where it goes.
  • Enterprise + HR-approved - the only place real employee names, performance data, or HR information belongs. Anything about a specific person lives here.
One-question test before you paste "Is there a real person's data in this, and would they be fine seeing where it went?" People-data - enterprise or approved tools only. Anonymized thinking - any assistant. That single question is rail two.
Self-studyThe 2026 landscape at a glance2 min read▶
  • Thinking + drafting: ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini for decisions, communication, planning, and synthesis.
  • In the flow of work: Copilot across M365/Teams, meeting notetakers that draft summaries and actions (session b7).
  • People + HR: only enterprise or HR-sanctioned tools touch employee data - the rest is off-limits for anything identifiable.
  • The shift: around 75% of companies now prioritize AI training for their leaders - the expectation has moved from "if" to "lead it well".
Part 3 · the habit every later session assumes

The two rails: you decide, you protect your people 4 min live

Everything else in this course is technique. These two rails are non-negotiable, and they are what separate a leader who gets sharper with AI from one who quietly hands away the parts of the job that were the point.

LiveRail 1 - AI advises, the leader decides and owns it2 min▶

AI can structure a decision, widen the options, and argue both sides. The judgment call, the accountability, and the human impact are yours and do not transfer. "The AI recommended it" is not leadership, and it is not a defence to your team, your boss, or yourself.

Real world

Don't outsource the judgment or the empathy. The two things AI cannot do are the two things leadership is made of: weighing values when the data is silent, and being present in a hard conversation. Use AI to prepare for both - and then walk in as a human, because a person on the other side can always tell the difference.

LiveRail 2 - protect your people's data2 min▶

Never put employee names, performance details, or sensitive HR information into a consumer AI tool, and stay alert to bias whenever AI touches a decision about a person - hiring, promotion, performance. Employment law and data-protection law (GDPR, PDPA) apply to your team's data exactly as they do to customers'.

  • Anonymize to think. You can use AI on a tricky people situation - strip the names and identifying details first.
  • Watch for bias in people decisions. AI can launder a biased pattern into a confident recommendation. A human owns every call about a person, with fairness front of mind.
  • Real data, safe tools only. Anything identifiable about an employee goes in enterprise or HR-approved tools, never a consumer chatbot.
Say it out loud once "AI advises, I decide and own it, and I protect my people's data." That sentence is the spine of the whole manager track.
Demo 1 of 2

Rubber-stamp vs advisor - see the difference ★ 12 min · everyone builds

The single most important habit in the course, in one experiment. Ask AI to weigh in on a real decision two ways - as a yes-man, then as an advisor - and watch a confident rubber-stamp become genuine counsel.

Pick a real, low-stakes decision you are weighing. Strip out any names or people-data (rail two).

Yes-man ask: "I am thinking of doing X - is that a good idea?" Note how quickly and agreeably it endorses you.

Advisor ask: reframe the real decision, ask for three options with tradeoffs, the strongest case against your favorite, a pre-mortem, and whose perspective is missing.

Compare. The second reads like counsel from someone who is not afraid of you - options, risks, blind spots - instead of a mirror. That delta is the whole value of AI in a decision.

Now make the call yourself, out loud: what you decide and why. AI structured it; you own it. That is the move you will engineer on purpose in b2.

Prompt A - yes-man (a confident rubber-stamp)I am thinking of restructuring my team this way: [plan]. That is a good idea, right?
★ Prompt B - advisor (genuine counsel)I am weighing how to solve [the real underlying problem]. One option is [plan]. Act as a sharp, candid advisor who is not afraid to disagree with me. Give me: (1) three genuine options with tradeoffs, including doing nothing; (2) the strongest case against my current favorite; (3) a pre-mortem - if this fails in six months, why; (4) whose perspective I am missing. Do not tell me I am right by default.
Why this is demo one Every later session is a variation on "make AI a candid advisor, then decide yourself". Decisions (b2), feedback (b5), and delegation (b6) are this same move. Learn it once tonight.
Demo 2 of 2

Write your team's AI ground rules for managers ★ 10 min · build your own

A reusable artifact you will keep: a short "how managers here use AI" card. You draft it with AI, then edit it - which is rail one in miniature.

Give the AI the two rails and your context: your team, the tools you have (or have not) approved, and the people-data you handle.

Ask for a one-page card with three lists: Green light (safe uses - decisions, drafts, synthesis, devil's advocate on anonymized inputs), Red light (never: employee data in consumer tools, letting AI decide about a person), and Always (make it argue back, human owns the call, protect people-data).

Edit it hard. Add your real tools and the lines your org draws. The AI draft is 70%; your judgement is the 30% that matters.

Share it with your peer managers. You will refine it in the leader session a4 (ethics) and the manager finale b10.

★ The ground-rules promptDraft a one-page "AI ground rules for managers" card for our team. Context: managers here handle team performance and some HR data; approved tools are [list, or "none yet"]. Build it around two rails: 1) AI advises, the manager decides and owns it - judgment, accountability, and people impact stay human; make AI argue back, do not let it rubber-stamp. 2) Protect people-data: no employee names, performance, or HR data in consumer tools; anonymize to think; watch for bias in people decisions. Format: three short lists - Green light, Red light, Always. Plain language, one page.
Real world

The card that kept managers confident and safe. A leadership team was split - some managers pasting team issues into free chatbots, others too scared to touch AI at all. A one-page card agreed in half an hour gave everyone a green light for the thinking and a hard line on people-data. Managers got sharper; nobody leaked a review.

AI is tuned to be helpful and agreeable, which removes the friction that makes counsel valuable. The fix is to make it argue against you on purpose.

2 · You want to think through a tricky situation with a specific team member. What do you do first?

Rail two: strip identifying people-data before using a consumer tool, and rail one: you own every decision about a person. AI helps you prepare, never decide.

Rail one. AI structures and drafts; the reading of people, the values call, the hard conversation, and owning the outcome are what leadership is made of.
