Delegation: automate, augment, keep human

Delegation used to mean handing work to people. Now you delegate across people and AI - and the manager's new skill is sorting the work correctly. Some of it is rote and rules-based; let tools do it. Some needs a human in the loop with AI assisting. And some must stay entirely human - judgment, relationships, the people calls. Tonight you sort your team's real work into those three buckets and design one augmented workflow end to end.

Part 0

Delegation just got a second dimension

For a manager, the question is no longer only "who does this?" but "should a human do this, should AI, or should they do it together?" Get the sort right and you free real capacity to move your people up the value chain. Get it wrong and you either automate something that needed a human, or you keep grinding on rote work AI could have taken. Tonight is about making that sort deliberately, with AI's help but your call.

Part 1 · covers management + automation canon

The three buckets 6 min live

Every task your team does lands in one of three buckets. Automate the rote and rules-based - let tools run it. Augment the work where AI assists but a human stays in the loop and owns the result. Keep human the judgment, relationships, creativity, and every people decision. Then reinvest the freed capacity in higher-value work.

LiveReading the three buckets3 min▶

The sort is not about how important a task is - it is about how much human judgment it needs. Ask that one question of every task and the bucket becomes obvious.

  • Automate - rote, repetitive, rules-based, low-judgment. Status roll-ups, formatting, routing, reminders. If a clear rule decides it, a tool can run it.
  • Augment - real value from speed, but a human must check and own the result. First-draft documents, analysis, option-generation, synthesis. AI drafts; a person decides.
  • Keep human - judgment, relationships, creativity, and every people decision. Feedback, coaching, trust-building, the hard calls. This is the work that was the point of the job.
The reallocation is the win Automating and augmenting is not the goal - it is the means. The goal is the capacity you free, redeployed to the keep-human work your team never had enough time for.
Self-studyThe sort question - one line per task2 min read▶

Run each task through this and the bucket falls out:

  • Does a clear rule decide the outcome? Yes - lean automate.
  • Does it need human judgment but benefit hugely from a first draft? Yes - augment, with a human owning the result.
  • Does it turn on trust, values, a relationship, or a call about a person? Yes - keep human, full stop.
Real world

The team that got its Fridays back. A manager sorted the team's recurring work and found three hours a week of status-report assembly sitting in the automate bucket. They automated the roll-up, moved the freed time into mentoring and customer conversations, and shipped the same reports with more time for the work that actually needed people.

Part 2 · covers workflow-design + automation practice

Designing an augmented workflow 6 min live

The augment bucket is where most of the near-term value lives, and it is the one that goes wrong without design. Pick an augment task, then decide four things: the handoff (AI drafts, human checks and owns), the guardrail that keeps it safe, the time saved, and where that time gets redeployed. The table pairs each bucket with what belongs in it and the caution that keeps it honest.

LiveBucket, what belongs, and the caution3 min▶
BucketWhat belongs + the caution
AutomateRules-based, repetitive tasks. Caution: only where a wrong output is cheap and visible - not silent, high-stakes decisions.
AugmentDrafting, analysis, synthesis. Caution: a named human checks and owns every output - "AI-assisted" is not "unchecked".
Keep humanJudgment, relationships, people calls. Caution: do not let convenience creep this work into the other buckets.
The handoffAI drafts, human reviews and decides. Caution: define who owns the result before the workflow runs, not after.
The redeployFreed time moves to higher work. Caution: name where it goes, or it quietly fills back up with more of the same.

The left column is the shape of the workflow you will design in demo two. The right column is what keeps an augmented workflow from turning into an unowned one.

Self-studyThe four questions of an augmented workflow2 min read▶

For any task you put in the augment bucket, design answers four questions:

  • Handoff - what does AI do, and where exactly does the human take over? AI drafts; the human checks and decides.
  • Guardrail - what keeps it safe? No people-data in the wrong tool, a required review step, a check for the failure mode that matters here.
  • Time saved - roughly how much, and be honest. If the review takes as long as doing it yourself, it does not belong in this bucket.
  • Redeploy - where does the freed time go? Name the higher-value work, or the gain evaporates.
Ownership is the guardrail that matters most An augmented workflow with no named owner is just unchecked output with extra steps. Decide who signs off before it runs.
Part 3 · the manager's new operating skill

Delegation to people AND AI 4 min live

The manager now delegates across two channels: to people and to AI. Done well, that is not a threat to your team - it is how you grow them, by moving them up the value chain as AI takes the rote off their plate.

LiveGrow people by moving them up the value chain2 min▶

When AI takes the rote work, the question is not "who do I need less of?" but "what higher work can this person now do?" The rote was rarely the reason you hired them. Delegating it to AI is a chance to delegate more meaningful work to your people.

  • Automate frees, it does not just cut. The capacity you free is the raw material for growth - if you deliberately reinvest it.
  • Move people up, not out. As AI takes the low-judgment tasks, hand your people more judgment, more ownership, more of the keep-human work.
  • You still own the sort. AI can help you think through what to automate and augment; the call about your people and their growth is yours.
Real world

The analyst who became an advisor. A manager automated the weekly data pull that ate a junior analyst's mornings. Instead of cutting the role, they redeployed the freed time into stakeholder conversations and recommendations. Within two quarters the analyst was presenting to leadership. Same person, higher work - because the rote went to a tool and the growth went to the human.

Self-studyDelegating to AI vs delegating to a person2 min read▶

The instinct is the same - hand off the work - but the follow-through differs:

  • To AI: define the task tightly, set the guardrail, and always keep a human owner. AI does not learn your standards over time the way a person does; you re-specify each time.
  • To a person: delegate the outcome, not just the task, and use the freed rote work as room for them to stretch. People grow into responsibility; that is the point.
  • The manager's job: route each piece of work to the right channel, keep ownership clear, and make sure the freed capacity turns into growth, not just more throughput.
Demo 1 of 2

Sort your team's real tasks into three buckets ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Bring your team's actual recurring work and use AI to help sort it - then make the calls yourself. The AI proposes a bucket for each task and explains why; you correct it, because you know the judgment each task really needs.

List your team's recurring tasks - the weekly and monthly work. Keep it to tasks and workflows, not people's names (rail two).

Ask AI to propose a bucket for each - automate, augment, or keep-human - with a one-line reason tied to how much judgment it needs.

Challenge every call. Where the AI says "automate", ask what breaks if it is wrong. Where it says "keep-human", confirm you agree.

Correct the sort. You know which "rote" task actually carries a quiet judgment, and which "human" task is really just formatting.

Circle the two or three biggest capacity wins - and name where the freed time will go. The sort is worthless without the redeploy.

Prompt A - the trap (letting AI make the call)Here is my team's task list. Tell me which ones to automate and I will set that up.
★ Prompt B - the move (AI proposes, you decide)Help me sort my team's recurring work into three buckets: AUTOMATE (rote, rules-based - a tool can run it), AUGMENT (AI assists, a human stays in the loop and owns the result), KEEP HUMAN (judgment, relationships, creativity, people decisions). Here are the tasks: [list them, no names]. For each, propose a bucket and a one-line reason based on how much human judgment it needs. Flag any you are unsure about and tell me what you would need to know. I will make the final call on each - I know the hidden judgment in these.
Why this is demo one The sort is the whole skill. Once your work is bucketed, everything else - what to automate, what to augment, where to grow your people - follows. Do it once here with your real tasks.
Demo 2 of 2

Design one augmented workflow, end to end ★ 8 min · build your own

Take one task from your augment bucket and design the whole workflow: the human-and-AI handoff, the guardrail, the time saved, and who owns the output. This is the artifact you carry out and try next week.

Pick one augment task - something with a real first-draft AI can produce and a human must check.

Design the handoff: what AI drafts, where the human takes over, and what "done" looks like at each step.

Set the guardrail and name the owner: the review step, the data line, and the person who signs off on the output.

Estimate the time saved honestly, and name exactly where that freed time gets redeployed. Write the workflow down.

★ The workflow-design promptHelp me design an augmented workflow for this task: [describe an augment-bucket task, e.g. drafting the weekly customer-update email from raw notes]. Design four things: 1) The handoff - what AI drafts, and exactly where a human takes over to check and decide. 2) The guardrail - what keeps it safe (data line, required review, the failure mode to catch). 3) The time saved - a rough, honest estimate. 4) The redeploy - where the freed time should go (higher-value work). Name a single human owner for the output. I will decide the final design.
Real world

The workflow that named its owner first. A team designed an augmented reporting workflow but skipped one question: who signs off? For a month, AI-drafted reports went out lightly checked because everyone assumed someone else had. One wrong number reached a client. They added a single named owner and a two-minute review step - and the workflow that had been quietly risky became genuinely faster and safe.

Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 30-45 min total

  • Finish both demos if you did not complete them live - especially naming where the freed capacity goes. The redeploy is the point, not the automation.
  • Run your designed augmented workflow on one real task this week and note whether the time-saved estimate held up.
  • Pick one person on your team and identify one piece of rote work you can take off their plate - and the higher work you will hand them instead.
  • Sanity-check your automate bucket: is anything in there actually a quiet judgment call that should be keep-human? Move it back.
  • Optional: catch yourself once about to keep grinding on rote work and ask "should this be in the automate bucket?"
