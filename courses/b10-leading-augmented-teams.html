Part 1 · covers exec-ed operating-model canon

The AI-augmented operating model 7 min live

An operating model is just the answer to "how does this team run?" made explicit. When AI is woven in well, four parts of your toolkit sit around the team's work and a cadence keeps them alive. Each part is a session you have already done - the finale is wiring them together.

LiveThe four parts, wired together4 min▶

Each part is a session you already have. The operating model is the sentence that connects them: "on our team, AI runs through the work like this."

  • Delegation buckets (b6). Every recurring task sorted into automate, augment, or keep-human. This is what AI touches and how.
  • Decision hygiene (b2). AI advises, widens options, argues both sides - then a human frames and owns the call. This is how AI touches decisions.
  • Enablement (b8). The team is coached up the value chain, with shared prompts and a rising floor of skill. This is how the team gets better, not just busier.
  • Guardrails (b9). People-data protected, bias checked, disclosure honored. This is the fence that keeps the whole thing safe.
The one-sentence test You have an operating model when you can say, in one breath: "AI handles this bucket of work, sharpens our decisions this way, we keep leveling the team up, we protect our people like this, and we review it on this rhythm." If you cannot say it yet, tonight is where you write it.
Self-studyWhy a model beats a pile of tips2 min read▶

A stack of clever AI tricks decays the moment attention moves on. A model persists because it is how the team runs, not something anyone has to remember to do. It survives a busy quarter, a new hire, and your own attention drifting elsewhere.

  • It is durable. Habits built into the workflow outlast enthusiasm.
  • It is teachable. A new team member can read one page and know how AI works here.
  • It is reviewable. Because it is explicit, you can check it against reality on a cadence and adjust.
Part 2 · covers outcome-measurement canon

Measuring whether it is actually better 5 min live

Here is where most AI programs quietly fail: they measure activity - prompts sent, tools logged into, hours "saved" on a slide - and mistake motion for progress. That is activity theater. Measure the things that would make you genuinely glad you did this, and nothing else.

LiveMeasure outcomes, not activity3 min▶

Tool usage is easy to count and tells you almost nothing. A team can be pasting into AI all day and be no better off - or worse. Point your scorecard at what actually changed for the work and the people.

Measure thisWhat it tells you
Outcomes - the work the team exists to deliverWhether AI improved results that matter, not effort
Capacity redeployed - hours freed and where they wentWhether saved time became higher-value work, not just idle savings
Quality - error rate, rework, decision qualityWhether faster came with better, or quietly worse
Team health - confidence, workload, moraleWhether people feel augmented, not surveilled or replaced
Tool usage / prompt counts (avoid)Almost nothing - this is activity theater, not impact
The test for a good measure Ask: "if this number went up, would I actually be happier?" Outcomes, redeployed capacity, quality, and team health pass. Prompt counts and login stats fail - a high number there could sit on top of worse work.
Self-studyA simple scorecard + a review cadence2 min read▶

You do not need a dashboard. Three or four honest measures and a standing rhythm to look at them beats a wall of metrics nobody trusts.

  • Pick 3-4 measures across outcomes, capacity, quality, and team health. Baseline each before you claim improvement.
  • Set a cadence - a monthly or quarterly review where you look at the scorecard, ask what the operating model should change, and keep the two rails on the agenda.
  • Mix the signals - some numbers, some qualitative. Team health especially lives in a conversation, not a metric.
Real world

The team that looked productive and delivered less. A group proudly reported AI usage climbing month over month - more prompts, more logins, more "adoption". But cycle time and quality had not moved, and two people quietly felt watched. The usage chart was theater. The moment they swapped it for outcomes, capacity, and a morale check, the real - and more modest - story showed up, and they could finally fix it.

Part 3 · the playbook you leave with

Sustaining it + the manager's playbook 4 min live

An operating model is not a launch, it is a practice. What keeps it alive is a rhythm and a handful of commitments you make as the leader. This is the playbook you walk out of the whole course holding.

LiveThe four commitments that keep it alive2 min▶

Sustaining is where good intentions usually die. Four commitments, on a cadence, keep the model from quietly reverting.

  • Hold the review cadence. A standing rhythm to look at the scorecard and adjust the model. If it is not on the calendar, it will not happen.
  • Keep the two rails loud. AI advises, you decide and own it; protect your people's data and check for bias. These do not fade as AI gets better - they matter more.
  • Grow people up the value chain. As AI takes the routine, keep moving the team toward judgment, relationships, and the work only humans do. That is the point of the freed capacity.
  • Revisit the model. Tools and tasks change. Re-sort the buckets, refresh the guardrails, and rewrite the one-pager when reality moves.
The playbook in one line Run the model, measure the outcomes, keep the rails, grow the people, and review on a cadence. That sentence is your manager's playbook for leading an AI-augmented team - and it is yours now.
Self-studyLeading people, still - the part that never automates2 min read▶

For ten sessions the throughline has been the same: AI changes the tools, not the job. The job is still reading your people, making the call when the data is silent, and being present in the hard conversation. Everything you built - the model, the scorecard, the guardrails - exists to protect the time and attention for exactly that.

  • The freed hours are for people. Redeploy capacity into coaching, connection, and judgment - not more throughput.
  • The rails are for people. Owning the decision and protecting the data are both, in the end, about the humans on your team.
  • You are still the leader. AI made you a sharper one. It never made you optional.

Build your team's AI operating model ★ 13 min · everyone builds

The capstone artifact. You will draft the one-page operating model for your real team - buckets, decision hygiene, enablement, guardrails, and a cadence - then edit it into something you would actually hand a new hire. This is nine sessions becoming one page.

Gather the raw material from earlier sessions: your delegation buckets (b6), your decision hygiene habit (b2), your enablement plan (b8), and your people-data + bias guardrails (b9). Anonymize anything about a specific person.

Use the prompt below to draft a one-page model that connects all four on a cadence. Give the AI your real team context.

Edit it hard. The draft will be generic. Make it yours: your actual tasks, your approved tools, your review rhythm, your team's names for things. The AI draft is 70%; your judgment is the 30% that matters - that is rail one, one last time.

Read it aloud. If you cannot say "this is how my team runs with AI" in one breath, tighten it until you can.

Share it with your team and your own manager. This is the document you lead from now.

★ The operating-model promptHelp me write a one-page AI-augmented operating model for my team. Context: [team, what we deliver, approved tools, review rhythm]. No real people-data below. Connect these four parts around our core work, on a cadence: 1) Delegation buckets - which recurring tasks we automate, augment, or keep fully human. 2) Decision hygiene - AI advises and widens options; a human frames and owns every call. 3) Enablement - how we coach the team up the value chain and share what works. 4) Guardrails - protect people-data, check for bias, disclose where AI affects a person. Then add a review cadence (how often we look at it and adjust). Format: one page, plain language, so a new team member could read it and know how AI works here. Do not tell me I am right by default - flag anything that looks thin or risky.
Why this is the finale Every earlier session gave you one instrument. Tonight you conduct. The operating model is the difference between a manager who tried some AI tricks and a leader who built a way of working that outlasts the hype.

Build an impact scorecard, not an activity tracker ★ 8 min · build your own

The model tells you how the team runs; the scorecard tells you if it is working. You will build a tight one - three to four outcome measures and a review cadence - and deliberately keep activity metrics off it.

List what your team actually exists to deliver, and how you would know it got better - faster, higher quality, or freeing people for higher-value work.

Use the prompt to draft 3-4 measures across outcomes, capacity redeployed, quality, and team health - with a baseline and a review cadence for each.

Run the theater test. Delete anything that is just activity (prompt counts, logins, tool adoption). For each survivor ask: "if this went up, would I actually be happier?"

Add it to your operating model as the review section, and put the first review on your calendar before you leave the room.

★ The scorecard promptHelp me build an impact scorecard for my AI-augmented team. Context: we deliver [core outcomes]; the team is [size / kind of work]. Give me 3-4 measures ONLY across these lenses: - Outcomes (the results the team exists to deliver) - Capacity redeployed (hours freed, and whether they went to higher-value work) - Quality (error rate, rework, decision quality) - Team health (confidence, workload, morale) For each: a plain definition, how to baseline it, and a review cadence. Explicitly EXCLUDE activity metrics - prompt counts, logins, tool-usage rates - and tell me why each excluded one is activity theater. Keep it to one page.
Real world

The scorecard that told the truth. One manager replaced a proud "AI adoption" chart with four honest measures - cycle time, hours redeployed into client work, rework rate, and a quarterly team-health check. The numbers were smaller and less flattering than the usage graph, but for the first time they were real. Knowing what was actually working, the team doubled down on the two moves that mattered and dropped the rest.

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What does an AI-augmented operating model include?

The model wires four parts of your toolkit - buckets (b6), decision hygiene (b2), enablement (b8), guardrails (b9) - around the work, held together by a review cadence. It is how the team runs, not a tool list.

2 · Your scorecard should measure...

Activity metrics are theater - a team can be busy on AI and no better off. Measure what would actually make you glad you did this: results, redeployed capacity, quality, and how the people feel.

3 · What keeps the operating model alive over time?

Sustaining is a practice, not a launch. Hold the cadence, keep the rails loud (AI advises, you decide and own it; protect your people), redeploy freed time into human work, and rewrite the model as reality changes.
