Part 0

Where AI helps a manager write

A blank page is where good messages go to die - so you rush, and rushed messages read cold. AI kills the blank page: it structures the hard message, compresses the pile into an update, and hands you a draft in seconds. Your job starts there. You bring the tone, the empathy, the one true detail, and the voice your team already trusts. This session is two rounds of exactly that - a tough message and an exec update - draft with AI, then own it.

Part 1 · covers management practice - written communication

AI drafts, you own the message 5 min live

Think of AI as the fastest first-draft engine you have ever had - and nothing more. It gets words on the page for updates, announcements, hard messages, and summaries. But a message is a relationship act, not a text-generation task. The tone, the empathy, and the trust are carried by you, and if the words sound like a machine wrote them, the reader feels it and trusts you a little less.

LiveDraft fast, then make it unmistakably yours3 min▶

The value of AI here is speed to a first draft, not the final words. The draft gets you past the blank page and gives you a clear structure to react to. Then you do the part only you can do: fit it to this reader, this moment, and your own voice. Ship the AI draft raw and people can tell - it is generic, a touch too polished, and strangely warm about nothing.

  • Use it to start, not to finish. A first draft in ten seconds beats a perfect blank page in ten minutes.
  • Do a voice pass, always. Read it aloud. Cut the words you would never say. Add the one specific detail only you know.
  • Sounding AI-written costs trust. Generic praise, hedge words, and tidy symmetry read as "did not actually think about me". That is the opposite of leadership.
★ Try it now (any chat AI) - the voice passHere is a draft message [paste it]. Rewrite it in a warmer, more direct human voice: shorter sentences, no corporate filler, no generic praise. Keep it honest. Flag any line that sounds AI-written so I can cut it.
Real world

The apology that read like a press release. A manager missed a commitment to their team, asked a chatbot for an apology note, and sent it almost unchanged. It was grammatically perfect and completely hollow - three sentences of polished nothing. One report replied "did you write this?" The fix took ninety seconds: name the specific miss, say sorry plainly, say what changes. AI can draft an apology; only you can mean it.

Self-studyThe tells of an AI-written message2 min read▶

Learn the tells so you can strip them out before you hit send.

  • Generic warmth. "I truly appreciate your incredible hard work" about nothing in particular. Real warmth names a real thing.
  • Tidy symmetry. Everything in threes, every paragraph the same length. People do not write that evenly.
  • Hedge soup. "It is important to note that we may want to consider" - three qualifiers before a simple point.
  • No specifics. Nothing only you would know: no name of the thing, the date, the actual number, the real reason.

The fix for all four is the same - one concrete detail and your own plain voice. That is the 30% AI cannot supply.

Part 2 · covers management practice - difficult conversations

Hard messages, done human 5 min live

The messages you dread - bad news, a boundary, a redirect - are exactly where AI helps most and where it must never be the voice. Use it for the scaffolding: clarity on the core point, a sane structure, and a heads-up on how the reader might react. Then rewrite it as you, out loud, warm and direct. And before you paste a word, anonymize any people-data - no names, no identifiable details, ever in a consumer tool.

LiveStructure the hard part, carry the humanity yourself3 min▶

A hard message fails in one of two ways: it buries the point so the reader is confused, or it lands the point so coldly the reader is hurt. AI is good at fixing the first - clear structure, the point up front, the likely objections anticipated. Only you can fix the second, because empathy is not a template. Split the work exactly that way.

  • Let AI structure. "What is the core message, what order, what will they push back on" - that scaffolding is safe to outsource.
  • You carry the empathy. The acknowledgement, the tone, the door left open - that is the relationship, and it is yours.
  • Anonymize first. Describe the situation as "a team member who..." with names and identifying details stripped. Rail two, every time.
Structure with AI, deliver as a human Ask AI to organize the message and flag reactions. Then close the chat and write the real thing in your voice. The reader can always tell which paragraph you actually meant.

Part 3 · covers management practice - executive communication

Exec updates + concision 4 min live

Your leadership does not have time for the pile - the thread, the doc, the five status notes. They need the compressed version: what happened, so what, what you need. AI is excellent at compression, which is a skill most managers find hard. Feed it the mess, get a crisp draft, then do the two things AI cannot - verify every fact, and own the framing so the "so-what" is the one you actually believe.

LiveWhat / so-what / ask - and verify the facts2 min▶

A good exec update answers three questions fast: what is the situation, why does it matter, and what do you need from me. AI can shape a pile into that structure in seconds. But compression is where errors hide - a summary can drop a caveat or overstate a number and sound perfectly confident doing it. So you verify before you send, and you own the framing.

  • Situation - what happened, in one or two lines. No backstory the exec does not need.
  • Impact - the so-what. Why it matters, to what, by how much. This is the line they actually read.
  • Ask - what you need. A decision, a resource, an escalation, or "no action, FYI". Be explicit.
Verify, then frame Check every number and claim against the source - AI will compress a maybe into a fact. Then set the framing yourself: the "so-what" is a judgment call, and it is yours to own.
Demo 1 of 2

Draft a tough message, then make it human ★ 13 min · everyone builds

Take a real message you are dreading and split the work: AI structures it and warns you about reactions, then you do an empathy and voice pass so it sounds like you and not a policy memo. Anonymize before you type a single name.

Pick a real hard message you owe someone - a decision they will not love, a boundary, a redirect. Strip all names and identifying details (rail two).

Structure pass: ask AI for the core message, the best order, and the two or three reactions to anticipate. Do not ask it to write the final note yet.

Draft pass: have it write a first version - clear, direct, kind. Read it. Notice what is useful and what sounds like a machine.

Empathy + voice pass: rewrite it yourself. Add the one real acknowledgement, cut the generic warmth, say it the way you would actually say it aloud.

Read the final version out loud. If it sounds like you talking to a person you respect, it is ready. That last pass is the whole job.

Prompt A - skip the structure, ship it raw (do not)Write a message telling a team member their project is being cancelled. Make it nice.
★ Prompt B - structure first, then you finish itI need to deliver a hard message to a team member (details anonymized): [situation, no names]. Do NOT write the final note. First give me: (1) the single core message they must not miss; (2) the best order to say it; (3) the two or three reactions I should anticipate and how to acknowledge each. Then draft one clear, kind, direct version I can react to - short sentences, no corporate filler, no generic praise. I will do the empathy and voice pass myself.
Real world

The redirect that kept a good person. A team lead had to tell a strong performer their pet project was being shelved. The AI structure pass surfaced the reaction they had not braced for - not anger, but feeling their judgment was doubted. That one insight reshaped the whole message: it opened by naming the good call behind the project, then explained the shift. The person stayed, and stayed engaged. AI spotted the reaction; the manager carried the care.

Demo 2 of 2

Messy bullets into a crisp exec update ★ 8 min · build your own

Turn a pile of status notes into a three-line update your leadership can read in fifteen seconds - situation, impact, ask - then verify every fact before it goes out.

Gather your raw material: the messy bullets, the thread, the half-written status. Remove anything that names a specific person if it is sensitive.

Ask AI to compress it into situation / impact / ask - crisp, exec-ready, no jargon, and to flag anything it was unsure about.

Verify pass: check every number, date, and claim against the source. Fix anything the compression overstated or dropped.

Framing pass: rewrite the "so-what" as the point you actually believe, and make the ask unmistakable - a decision, a resource, or "FYI, no action".

★ The exec-update promptTurn these raw notes into a crisp executive update [paste bullets]. Format it as exactly three parts: 1) Situation - what happened, one or two lines. 2) Impact - the so-what: why it matters, to what, by how much. 3) Ask - the one thing I need (a decision, a resource, an escalation, or "FYI, no action"). Plain language, no jargon. Flag any number or claim you are unsure about so I can verify it.
Own the so-what AI can arrange the facts, but "why this matters" is a judgment call. Read the impact line and ask: is that the point I would stake my name on? If not, rewrite it. That line is yours.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Why do you always do a voice pass on an AI-drafted message?

AI gets you a fast first draft, but the reader trusts you, not the tool. Generic warmth and no specifics read as "did not think about me". The voice pass is where you own the message.

2 · How should you use AI on a hard message about a specific team member?

Split the work: AI does the scaffolding on an anonymized description, you carry the empathy and deliver it as yourself. Never put a real name or people-data in a consumer tool.

3 · What is the discipline for an AI-compressed exec update?

Compression is where errors hide - a caveat drops, a number inflates, and it all sounds sure. You verify against the source and own the "so-what", because why-it-matters is a judgment call.
